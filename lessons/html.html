Lesson 1

Words on a page

HTML is the structure of everything you read in a browser. It is text, wrapped in labels called tags, and the browser turns it into a page.

A tag is a label in angle brackets

Most tags come in pairs: an opening tag like <p> and a closing tag like </p>. Whatever sits between them is a piece of content the browser knows how to treat. 

<p>Hello, world.</p>

That is a paragraph. Change the tag and you change the meaning — and the look — without touching the words inside.

The handful you will use constantly

  • <h1> to <h6> — headings, from most to least important.
  • <p> — a paragraph of text.
  • <a href="..."> — a link to another page.
  • <ul> and <li> — a bulleted list and its items.
  • <img src="..." alt="..."> — an image, with a description for screen readers.

Tags nest like boxes

A page is a tree of elements. A list holds items, items hold text. Keep opening and closing tags in order and the tree stays sane. 

<ul>
  <li>HTML</li>
  <li>CSS</li>
  <li>JavaScript</li>
</ul>
